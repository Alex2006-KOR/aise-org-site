::: lead

## The six-stage cycle {#cycle}

The constitution's §4 sets the organization's life as a cycle of six stages: recruitment, assignment, collaboration, reporting, feedback and
knowledge accumulation. Experience gathered in the last stage grounds the next recruitment judgment (can an existing role absorb the need?),
closing the cycle.

## Growth accumulates in the organization {#growth}

At first the picture was of roles improving themselves through the knowledge gained on projects. That approach was dropped. Growth accumulates
not in individual roles but in what the organization can do and in the common templates.

- **The portfolio.** The list of reusable roles, workflows and insights, promoted on the principle "prove it first, then promote".
- **The common base and templates.** Strengthened in Meta mode.
- Both take their material from the retrospective when a department ends. Before a department closes, the ops staff writes a retrospective
  and judges whether anything should go into the portfolio, and the HR staff reviews the roles that department used. Failed or abandoned
  departments do not skip this review.

<Cite ids="op-growth,op-close" />

::: point Details — why people's growth was not modeled (background · problem · solution) {#people-growth}
**Background.** A company's growth includes both the company's own growth and its members' growth. The first idea was a structure in which the
"person" recruited into a role grows with the knowledge gained on projects.

**Problem.** That would make a role's definition file grow longer and longer: a way of raising fixed cost, and not all of it is needed for every
task. The same question came back twice: now that several departments use the same role, is it time to build individual proficiency? It was
looked at again, but several departments had used the same class for different one-off work; no record showed friction from doing the same kind
of work twice.

**Solution.** Roles are recruited and fired. A department requests the people it actually needs, and the HR staff researches and approves.
The context each department needs is attached to that department's records as a domain profile. Managing people is the HR staff's job, so the PM
does not carry "people's minds and growth". Individual proficiency was deferred twice, and the condition for revisiting it was narrowed to "a
second run visibly re-deriving what a first run already found, seen in the records".

<Cite ids="op-people-growth,role-proficiency" />
:::

::: point Details — status: the structure is open, few promotions so far
As of 2026-10-27 the portfolio holds 5 roles and no workflows or insights. There are 2 retrospectives.

<Cite ids="portfolio-index" />
:::

## An organization, not a framework {#goal}

aise-core is not a project to build a multi-agent framework. The constitution's §8 sets the goal as an organization operating model that uses AI
to learn on its own, grow on its own and keep accumulating organizational capability. A framework has to be re-tuned for every new model; an
organization should grow more capable over time, with operating principles that hold as new technology arrives. What outlasts a change of model
is the decisions and experience accumulated in the organization's records ([figure 3-3](/en/story/principles#ai-native)).

## Limits {#limits}

Failures met along the story sit in their chapters as the reason a mechanism exists. Only what is still unsolved is gathered here.

- **Not yet tested with other tools.** There is one adapter, for Claude Code, and aise-core has not been distributed to anyone else.
  → [chapter 8](/en/story/distribution)
- **"The same user experience after distribution" is not tested with another user.** What must be the same is guaranteed at the policy layer
  (hooks, scripts, policy documents) and the organization is meant to differ, but whether that design actually gives the same experience has not
  been checked. → [chapter 8](/en/story/distribution#not-yet-distributed)
- **Independent re-verification has not caught up.** The share of done items that are re-verified is being raised.
  → [chapter 7](/en/story/verification#reverify)
- **The growth structure is open, but few promotions have happened.** 5 roles, 0 workflows or insights.

<Cite ids="op-limits" />

::: point Details — what was given up {#given-up}
- **Debate on one level.** Given up in choosing the vertical structure. The operator talking only with the staff is also a choice opinions can
  differ on. Both are structural choices, not locks, and can be re-evaluated and changed. → [chapter 2](/en/story/at-a-glance#given-up)
- **Some predictability.** A chart drawn in advance shows at a glance what the organization has. Recruiting only when needed leaves that picture
  always incomplete.
- **An individual growth curve.** There is no guarantee a role is faster the second time. Organizational knowledge stands in for it, but it is
  not the same as individual proficiency: a deliberately unsolved state.
- **Fine-grained permission control.** There is no mechanism such as "this role cannot read this folder". If needed, it is to be built at the
  OS or infrastructure layer, not with hooks; an application-layer hook is inherently best-effort, and no real work has asked for it yet.
- **Some duplicated effort.** Re-deriving from the records each time means re-thinking what an earlier run already thought.
  → [chapter 6](/en/story/operation#stateless)

<Cite ids="op-vertical,folder-access,role-proficiency" />
:::
