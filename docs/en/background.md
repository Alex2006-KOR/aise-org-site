# Background & Philosophy

This category only covers **why**. What the structure looks like and how it actually runs is
handed off to the next category ([How It Works](/en/how-it-works)); here we follow the judgment
that led to building such a structure in the first place.

## Why read them in this order

The three pages narrow the same question step by step.

1. **[Five principles, and why each one was needed](/en/philosophy)** — first there was a
   decision: build not "a better way to use AI" but "an organization where AI works." Follow how
   five principles branch out of that single choice. Skip this page and every design that follows
   just looks like a matter of taste.
2. **[What the usual way does differently](/en/real-world-vs-aise)** — read alone, principles
   sound like nice words. So immediately after, we line up **where those principles actually
   diverge from the way things are usually done**, across seven axes. Only here does it become
   visible that the previous page's five sentences were choices that cost something.
3. **[What we don't have to give up because it's AI](/en/ai-native-principles)** — some of those
   differences aren't "we just decided to do it differently"; they're **only possible because AI
   isn't a person.** Those get collected separately, at the end. Without that distinction it's
   easy to over-generalize things that should never be carried back into a human organization.

In one line: **the decision → the differences that decision created → which of those differences
AI made possible.**

## When it's fine to skip this category

If you'd rather go "enough philosophy, show me the structure," go straight to
[How It Works](/en/how-it-works). We'd still suggest glancing at the comparison table in
[What the usual way does differently](/en/real-world-vs-aise) on the way — the reason the later
designs have exactly the shape they have is compressed into that one table.
